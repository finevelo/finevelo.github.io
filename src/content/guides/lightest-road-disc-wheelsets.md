The lightest wheelset in our database is the **Scope Artech 4 at a claimed 1,120 g**, a 45 mm deep hooked rim. Roval's Alpinist CLX III (1,131 g, 33 mm) comes second. Both are roughly 170–180 g lighter than Shimano's new carbon-spoked Dura-Ace R9370 C50 (1,302 g), the groupset-maker benchmark.

**Weights are not directly comparable.** Some brands quote with tubeless tape and valves fitted (Roval, Enve, Zipp's 353 NSW), others without (Zipp's 454 NSW, Cadex's Max 50). Cadex's figures show the gap: 1,250 g without, 1,290 g with. Where an independent scale exists, we note it. Those figures usually land 10–70 g above the claim.

Three trends show up in the table:

- **Carbon spokes** have reached the top end: Roval, Cadex, Lightweight, Black Inc and now Shimano's Dura-Ace R9370 use them. Enve's flagship SES 6.7 Pro still uses steel.
- **Hooked or hookless** is not settled. Zipp's NSW wheels (capped at 73 psi / 5 bar) and Cadex's Max 40 and Max 50 are hookless, as is Enve's SES 4.5 Pro, whose 0.5 mm mini hook is rated to 100 psi. Enve's SES 6.7 Pro, DT Swiss, Roval, Scope, Campagnolo, Shimano and Black Inc use hooked rims.
- **Depth is capped.** Since 1 January 2026, the UCI no longer allows rims deeper than 65 mm in mass-start road races, which is why Enve's SES 6.7 Pro is now 59/65 mm.

Factor wanted to find out how quick a road bike can get while staying within UCI rules, and the ONE is its answer. Factor announced the bike in December 2025 and calls it the fastest UCI-legal road bike in the world. Its launch material gives neither a price nor a weight, so both are TBC here. What it does give is a detailed account of the front end, the geometry and the wind-tunnel numbers behind that claim.

## What is the Factor ONE?

It is an aero race bike designed around how professionals now sit. Factor says elite riders have moved further forward and use narrower bars, shorter cranks and larger tyres. It says the ONE was built for this from the start rather than adapted to it.

The brief came from Alex Dowsett. According to Factor, he asked for a "lead-out bike": something that would let the last man hold the front for 200 to 300 metres in the final kilometre.

## What makes the front end different?

The ONE's front end pairs a bayonet-style fork with a fairing Factor calls the "chin". Factor says recent UCI rule changes made the structure possible and that its engineers worked closely with the UCI during development. It adds that the shape went through hundreds of CFD iterations.

The aim is to manage airflow where it first meets the bike, reducing spillover drag. Factor claims the design keeps its advantage at higher yaw angles, where it says rival frames lose efficiency.

## How does the geometry handle extreme positions?

Factor has separated the handlebar mount from the steering axis. The brand says this allows reach positions that would previously have required unstable stems. It has also revised the bottom-bracket drop to restore handling balance, which it says shifts with shorter cranks and modern tyre choices.

## How does the cockpit adjust?

There is no stem. Instead, the integrated bar comes in five sizes (110–150 mm stem equivalents) and three high-rise options, with up to 15 mm of spacers. Factor puts the total stack range at 35 mm. In practice, fit is set by choosing from that parts menu rather than by swapping a stem.

## How fast does Factor say it is?

Factor says the ONE was tested in the wind tunnels at Guelph and SASI, with these results:

| Compared with | Factor's claimed advantage |
|---|---|
| Factor OSTRO VAM 2.0 | More than 8% faster |
| Cervélo S5 (2024) | About 15% faster |
| Specialized Tarmac SL8 | More than 22% faster |
| Yaw behaviour | Keeps its advantage beyond 15° |

These are Factor's figures. The release does not give test speeds, rider setup or wheel choices, which makes direct comparison with other published data difficult.

## Has it been raced?

Only briefly, Factor says, but with a result. The bike's first appearance was as a prototype in 2025, and it won straight away: Jake Stewart took the fifth stage of that year's Critérium du Dauphiné, with the finish in Mâcon, crossing the line in front of Jonathan Milan and Mathieu van der Poel. Lily Williams of Human Powered Health also raced the bike briefly in 2025.

## Who is it for?

On Factor's own description, the ONE targets riders who prioritise sustained high speed. That means sprinters, lead-out riders and anyone racing in crosswinds or echelons. It also suits riders who already use, or want, an aggressive forward position, which is the fit the geometry was designed around.

Two gaps remain for buyers. Without a published weight, riders who race on hilly courses cannot yet judge the trade-off. And because the cockpit is stemless, anyone who likes to fine-tune their position often should look closely at the bar size and rise options before committing.

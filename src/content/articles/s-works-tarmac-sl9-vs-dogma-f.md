Specialized launched the **S-Works Tarmac SL9** around **30 June 2026**, days before the Tour de France. With Shimano Dura-Ace Di2 it lists at **$13,499.99** (€13,999 / £11,999), and Specialized claims **6.6 kg** for a size 56. The current **Pinarello Dogma F** was launched in June 2024. Pinarello's US site shows no price (launch pricing was $14,500), but a US retailer lists the 2026 Dura-Ace Di2 build at **$15,750**, and Pinarello claims **6.77 kg** for a size 53.

That makes the Specialized about $2,250 cheaper and, on paper, the lighter bike too. Until June, this comparison would have been against the Tarmac SL8. It is now the outgoing model.

## What changed from the SL8 to the SL9?

The SL9 is an evolution rather than a new concept. Specialized claims:

- **Aero:** 4 W less at 45 km/h than the SL8. Specialized points to a 4 mm narrower "Speed Sniffer" head tube (10% less frontal area) and a "Win Fin" shape behind the seat tube, which it says is worth 0.5 W on its own. In TOUR's wind tunnel, about 2 W of the 4 W came from the new Roval wheels.
- **Weight:** a claimed **687 g** FACT 12r frame in size 56, against 685 g claimed for the SL8. The frame did not get lighter; Specialized's case rests on the complete bike and on aero.
- **Geometry and ride:** unchanged stiffness and compliance targets, and the same geometry as the SL8.
- **Tyres:** clearance stays at 32 mm. The bike now ships on 30 mm tyres.

Specialized's headline metric is "Time to Finish", a course simulation that combines drag, mass and rolling resistance. It is not a wind-tunnel figure alone. Escape Collective reads the launch the same way: the SL9 is not built to win the wind-tunnel table.

The weight comparison is less clear-cut than the table suggests. The claims refer to different sizes, and the measured figures come from different scales and build specs. What can be said is that both bikes sit at or near the UCI's 6.8 kg minimum. A WorldTour team gains nothing from going lighter.

The geometry differs by millimetres. In size 56, the Tarmac is 5 mm lower and 4 mm longer than the Dogma F. That puts it marginally more stretched out, but either bike fits the same rider with a stem change. The bigger fit difference is at the front: the Dogma ships with Pinarello's own integrated Talon Ultra Fast cockpit, which flares 7° at the drops.

## What do the reviewers say?

- **TOUR** gave the S-Works SL9 its top grade as a race bike. It praised the same stability and precision as before.
- **BikeRadar** rode the Red AXS build and called it a light, engaging all-rounder. Its tester found the gains over the SL8 hard to feel on the road, and listed price and 32 mm clearance as drawbacks.
- For the Dogma F, the verdicts from our [Dogma F analysis](/news/pinarello-dogma-f-2026/) still stand. Cyclingnews scored it 94%, and both BikeRadar and road.cc single out its handling. The common criticisms are price and limited tyre clearance.

## Who races them?

- **Specialized:** at the 2026 Tour, Red Bull–BORA–hansgrohe raced the SL9 in road stages. Soudal Quick-Step remain on Specialized through 2026. Escape Collective reported in April that the team will switch to Merida for 2027; the team did not comment.
- **Pinarello:** Netcompany INEOS have ridden Pinarello since the team's Team Sky days. Since 2026, Pinarello–Q36.5 have raced the Dogma F as well, with Tom Pidcock.

## Which one should you buy?

On the numbers, the SL9 wins: it is lighter, cheaper and clears wider tyres. The Dogma F wins on handling, which reviewers consistently rank among the best, and on the fact that it is a Pinarello. If you already own an SL8, the SL9 offers little you will feel; BikeRadar's tester reached the same conclusion. Discounted SL8s at outgoing-model prices may be the better value of the three.

The Spectral:ON CF LTD is the top model in Canyon's fourth-generation Spectral:ON CF range, presented on 12 November 2026. Canyon describes it as the product manager's "dream bike" and says only 150 will be built. Claimed weight is 20.8 kg (45.8 lb). Price is TBC: Canyon's release does not state one.

## What is the Spectral:ON CF LTD?

It is a full-power electric trail bike built around a carbon frame and Avinox's M2S motor. Canyon's stated aim for the whole Spectral:ON CF line is to close the gap between heavy full-power e-MTBs, which offer torque, and lighter SL bikes, which offer handling. The LTD is the top-of-the-range version of that idea.

## How much does it cost, and when can you get one?

Canyon has not published a price in its release, so we list it as TBC. The company says the LTD can be ordered in most markets in December, with shipping expected from January 2027.

For the range as a whole, Canyon's October release gave pre-orders from 4 December and deliveries from 19 January. It also said selected bikes would reach the USA and Australia in early 2027. Canyon has not said whether the LTD is among those.

## What powers it?

Canyon rates the Avinox M2S motor at 150Nm of torque and 1,500W of peak power. The brand says that is nearly double what full-power e-MTBs offered a few years ago.

The motor is paired with Avinox's FP700 battery, a 700Wh unit that Canyon credits with keeping weight down and the frame slim. Canyon says the full 1,500W peak is available in all five assist modes.

## What is notable about the frame?

The full-carbon frame weighs a claimed 2.9 kg (6.3 lb). According to Canyon, it is tested to Category 4e, the enduro-level strength and impact standard. Canyon says it tuned the layup for a stiffer front triangle and more compliant seat and chainstays.

Geometry draws on the Strive:ON and the Spectral CF: a 64-degree head angle and a 78.5-degree seat angle, with a longer rear centre. Chainstay length is size-specific, shorter on Small and Medium than on Large and X-Large. Canyon says the range ships with mixed 29/27.5in wheels, and a flip-chip allows a full 29in setup.

The suspension has a revised leverage curve and more anti-squat and anti-rise. Canyon says this improves pedalling efficiency, mid-stroke support and stability under braking.

On the workshop side, brake lines are fully guided from a head-tube port to a chainstay port, without removing the motor or battery.

## Who is it for?

On Canyon's own description, it suits riders who want full-power assistance for long or steep days but would rather not carry the weight usually attached to it.

The build sheet points to buyers who want the most complete version straight from the box: SRAM's top electronic drivetrain, Maven Ultimate brakes and carbon DT Swiss wheels. We have not ridden it, so whether the handling matches Canyon's claims remains open until we do.

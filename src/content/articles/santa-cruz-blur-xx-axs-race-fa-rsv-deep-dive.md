The Blur XX AXS Race FA RSV sits at the top of Santa Cruz's cross-country range. It costs $13,399 in the US, and Santa Cruz claims a weight of 11.44kg (25.23lb), without stating the frame size. It is a 120mm-travel 29er built around SRAM's XX Eagle AXS T-Type drivetrain, with RockShox Flight Attendant on both fork and shock.

## What is the Blur 5?

The Blur 5 is the fifth generation of Santa Cruz's XC platform. The brand pitches it at both XCO racing and the "days between the races", describing it as a lightweight short-travel trail bike as much as a race machine. Santa Cruz says it increased travel, revised the geometry and reworked the suspension kinematics compared with the Blur 4.

Every Blur 5 frame now uses Santa Cruz's higher-grade CC carbon, from the $4,899 Deore build upwards. The company says it slimmed the tubes and trimmed nearly every carbon ply to save weight while hitting specific stiffness targets, and the frame keeps the lifetime warranty.

Flight Attendant is the defining feature. Santa Cruz says the system "automatically adjusts" the suspension to the trail and the rider. Like the other Race kits, this build also gets faster-rolling tyres and shock tunes that Santa Cruz says prioritise efficiency and support.

The step from the XTR RSV is $3,100. That build pairs Shimano XTR with Fox Factory suspension. The XX build switches to SRAM's XX AXS drivetrain with a power meter, RockShox Flight Attendant suspension and the race-specific setup. The GX AXS Race RSV is the cheaper race option: Santa Cruz calls it race trim "without the robots", using remote lockouts instead of automatic ones.

The chainstay grows with frame size. Santa Cruz achieves this by moving the pivot locations in the front triangle, using a single swingarm. It says the geometry is designed around a 120mm fork with 44mm offset, and that up to 130mm is acceptable.

## Which practical details matter?

- **Storage:** a downtube Glovebox with a tube purse and tool wallet.
- **Bottles:** two mounts, on the seat tube and the downtube.
- **Routing:** tube-in-tube for dropper, derailleur and brake, with no through-headset routing.
- **Standards:** 73mm threaded BB, 55mm chainline, and a UDH-compatible frame that comes with Santa Cruz's own machined aluminium hanger.
- **Limits:** 2.4in maximum rear tyre, and 180mm rear rotors only.
- **Rider weight:** 108kg for the complete bike, set by the fork; 123kg for the frame.
- **Support:** lifetime warranty and free pivot bearings for the original owner.

## Who is it for?

This build is for riders who race XC and want automatic suspension and power data without adding them afterwards. The 60mm stem and flat bar reflect that brief.

Riders who mostly ride rather than race may be better served elsewhere in the range. Santa Cruz says its non-Race kits use slightly more trail-oriented suspension, grippier tyres, 50mm stems and riser bars. We have not ridden the bike, so how well Flight Attendant justifies its premium remains an open question.

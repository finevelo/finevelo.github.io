The **S-Works Turbo Creo 2** launched in September 2023 at **$14,000 / £12,000 / €13,000**. Three years later it is still Specialized's flagship drop-bar e-bike, now listed at **$14,399.99** in the US. Specialized claims **12.87 kg** for a 2026 size 56 (12.96 kg at launch); BikeRadar weighed one at **13.13 kg**.

There is no Creo 3. Retailers already list 2027 Creo 2 builds. The more interesting question is why, three years on, no other brand sells a direct equivalent.

## What does $14,399.99 buy?

| | S-Works Turbo Creo 2 |
|---|---|
| Motor | Specialized SL 1.2, 50 Nm, 320 W |
| Battery | 320 Wh internal; 160 Wh range extender available |
| Drivetrain | SRAM RED AXS E1 levers and brakes, XX1 Eagle AXS derailleur, XG-1295 10-50t cassette (mullet) |
| Wheels | Roval Terra CLX II |
| Tyres | Tracer TLR All Terrain, 700x50c (2026; 2024 bikes had Tracer Pro 700x47c) |
| Front suspension | Future Shock 3.3 |
| Seatpost | SRAM Reverb AXS XPLR wireless dropper |
| Tyre clearance | 57 mm |
| Weight | 12.87 kg claimed (56, 2026); 13.13 kg measured (BikeRadar, 2024 bike) |

On Specialized's own product page the drivetrain is RED AXS E1 levers with an XX1 Eagle AXS mountain bike rear end; only the dropper post carries the XPLR name. The stock tyre grew from a 47 mm Tracer Pro on 2024 bikes to a 50 mm Tracer TLR All Terrain on the 2026 model.

## What changed from the first Creo?

The Creo 2 turned an e-road bike into an e-gravel bike:

- **Tyre clearance** went from 42 mm to 57 mm, and the geometry was reworked for gravel.
- **The motor** is the SL 1.2, which Specialized rates at 33% more power and 43% more torque than the SL 1.1 in the first Creo SL.
- **The frame** is claimed to be about 120 g lighter.
- **A dropper post** and the damped **Future Shock 3.3** come on the S-Works.

## Who are its rivals?

Measured against the S-Works, every rival falls short on at least one of weight, tyre clearance or price class.

| Bike | Motor | Battery | Price | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S-Works Turbo Creo 2 | Specialized SL 1.2, 50 Nm | 320 Wh | $14,399.99 | 12.87 kg claimed |
| Cervélo Rouvida (Red XPLR AXS 1) | Fazua Ride 60, 60 Nm | 432 Wh | $13,000 (launch, 2023) | 12.38 kg claimed (road build) |
| Trek Checkpoint+ SL 7 AXS | TQ HPR60 | 360 Wh | $7,999.99 | 14.05 kg (ML) |
| Pinarello Nytro Gravel | TQ HPR50, 50 Nm | 360 Wh | €13,000 (launch, 2023) | TBC |
| BMC URS AMP ONE | TQ HPR60 | 360 Wh | CHF 7,999 | 14.9 kg |
| Orbea Denna | Shimano EP801, 85 Nm | 420 Wh | €9,999 (M11e) | 13.9 kg claimed (M20i, L) |

The Cervélo comes closest on price and weight. Its gravel version clears 43.5 mm tyres, though, not 57 mm. Trek's Checkpoint+ is the closest in concept, at about half the price. Orbea's Denna uses a full-power motor and belongs to a different class.

## What do the reviewers say?

- **road.cc**, in a first-ride review of the Creo 2 Expert, called it a genuine e-gravel bike that shows its true nature on rough gravel. Its criticisms: the motor is still more audible than rivals', and the price is high.
- **Cycling Weekly** gave the Expert Carbon 4.5 out of 5 and named the Turbo Creo 2 the best overall pick in its 2026 e-gravel buyer's guide.
- **BikeRadar** weighed and photographed the S-Works for its Bike of the Week feature.

## Where does it look dated?

- **Battery size.** At 320 Wh it has less capacity than the 360 Wh TQ-powered rivals and the Rouvida's 432 Wh.
- **Motor noise.** road.cc's criticism has not gone away.
- **Cheaper builds have moved on.** Specialized added an aluminium Comp E5 in 2024, and the 2026 Creo 2 Expert switched to Shimano GRX Di2. The S-Works has had only detail updates: RED AXS E1 levers and brakes, and 50 mm tyres.

## The verdict

The S-Works Turbo Creo 2 is still the only lightweight e-bike that combines a sub-13 kg claim with 57 mm tyres and a dropper post. That explains why Specialized has felt no pressure to replace it. At $14,399.99 it is hard to justify over the Expert. Specialized's US sale price of $12,799.99 suggests the market agrees.

*Figures come from Specialized, BikeRadar, road.cc, Cycling Weekly, TOUR and manufacturer pages for the rivals. Rival prices are list prices for the builds named.*

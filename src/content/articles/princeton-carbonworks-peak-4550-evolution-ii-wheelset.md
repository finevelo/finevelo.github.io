Princeton CarbonWorks' Peak 4550 Evolution II is the brand's lightweight, climbing-focused road wheelset. The company's web store lists it at 3,150 to 4,500 (the product page shows no currency symbol), with a claimed weight of 1200g in its lightest configuration. That places it firmly in the premium wheelset bracket, and the details explain where the money goes.

## What is the Peak 4550 Evolution II?

Princeton describes it as a wheel that "excels on all terrains": light for climbing, but intended to stay composed on descents and stable on flatter roads. It sits in the same Evolution II family as the Wake 6560 Evolution II, and Princeton says the Peak draws on the improvements made to that wheel.

The product page does not state the rim depth, so we will not guess at it here.

## What has changed versus the previous generation?

Princeton says the internal rim width has grown to 22mm, with a 32mm maximum external width, while rim weight has dropped to a claimed 390g. According to the brand, each rim is balanced at the factory to suit the tubeless valves supplied with the set.

The Evolution II family adds four colourways to the six standard finishes, for ten in total.

## Why does Princeton stick with a hooked rim?

This is the most opinionated part of the product. The Peak keeps a hooked, hole-less, tubeless-ready tyre bed that Princeton says accepts tubed or tubeless tyres, needs no rim tape and carries no tyre pressure restrictions.

Princeton openly argues against hookless designs, which it concedes are cheaper to make and lighter, but which it calls "problematic and potentially dangerous" with regard to tyre choice and pressure limits. That is the manufacturer's position, and it frames the trade-off clearly: tyre and pressure freedom against some extra weight.

Princeton also says the wheel passes 120J in impact testing.

## How much does it weigh with each hub?

Every build uses Alpina Hyperlite spokes and Sapim SecureLock alloy nipples. Weight differences come down to the hub. Princeton's claimed wheelset weights:

The spread between lightest and heaviest is 205g. Princeton gives a price range rather than per-hub prices on the page, so the cost of each specific build is TBC.

## Which finishes are available?

The standard range covers six options. Black and white are each offered in both a matte and a gloss finish, while the gold and chrome versions come in gloss only. On top of those, the Evolution II family brings four further gloss colours: sand, cobalt, red and chameleon.

## Who is it for?

On Princeton's own framing, the Peak 4550 Evolution II is for riders who climb a lot but do not want to give up stability elsewhere. The specification points to a buyer who values choice: six hub options, three freehub standards, ten finishes, and a rim that Princeton says does not restrict tyre selection or pressure.

It is also for riders who share, or at least accept, Princeton's case for hooked rims. If the lowest possible weight is the only goal, Princeton itself admits hookless rims can be lighter. If tyre freedom and a tape-free tubeless setup matter more, the Peak is built around that priority.

We have not ridden the Peak 4550 Evolution II, so performance claims above are Princeton's own.

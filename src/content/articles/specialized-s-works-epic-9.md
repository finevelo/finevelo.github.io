Specialized launched the Epic 9 in **April 2026**, two seasons after the Epic 8. The **S-Works Epic 9 costs $15,249.99 / €14,499** with SRAM XX SL Eagle Transmission and RockShox Flight Attendant. According to Singletracks, that is about $750 more than the S-Works Epic 8 cost at launch. Specialized claims **1,589 g** for a size M frame including shock, axle and hardware. Pinkbike weighed a complete XL S-Works at **10.1 kg**.

Both bikes have 120 mm of travel front and rear. The Epic 9 is the lighter, more race-focused successor; Pinkbike's first-ride headline calls it "unapologetically race-focused".

The Ultralight LTD is cheaper than the standard S-Works, which is unusual for the lightest model in a range. In the UK the S-Works frameset is listed at £5,999. In the US, Singletracks reported the Comp and Sport builds as later additions that bring the range below $5,000.

## What does it weigh?

- **Frame (claimed):** 1,589 g, size M, including the shock, seat collar, axle and hardware.
- **Saving vs Epic 8 (claimed):** Specialized's own product page says 206 g, and BIKE gives 206 g (about 14%) for the frame; Bikerumor calls the 206 g the "total system". Bikerumor attributes 110 g to the front triangle, 37 g to the rear triangle, 17 g to the seat tube and 15 g to the main pivot. Singletracks reports a 179 g saving instead, so check which figure an outlet quotes.
- **Complete, S-Works (measured):** 10.1 kg by Pinkbike, size XL.
- **Complete, Ultralight LTD (claimed):** sources conflict. Velomotion gives 8.66 kg (M), Specialized's US page 8.56 kg, and several headlines about 8.5 kg. We list it as TBC until it is confirmed.

## What changed from the Epic 8?

The Epic 8 already dropped the Brain shock that defined the Epic for two decades. The Epic 9 keeps electronic Flight Attendant control on the S-Works and focuses on weight and details:

- **Suspension:** an updated shock linkage that Specialized says cuts suspension friction by 11%.
- **Geometry:** the head angle is 65.9° in the low setting and 66.3° in the high setting (flip chip). The seat angle is 76° and reach on a size M is 450 mm. Outlets disagree on how these compare with the Epic 8, so we have left that comparison out.
- **Tyres:** the S-Works build ships on 2.35 in Specialized tyres. Pinkbike says rear tyre clearance has been significantly increased; we have not found a maximum tyre size from Specialized.
- **Storage:** the internal SWAT down-tube storage is gone; Specialized's spec sheet lists a removable, bolt-on SWAT Box 2.0 instead.

## Has it won anything?

The Epic 9's first season has gone very well for Specialized's women's team:

- **Sina Frei** won the World Cup XCO and XCC at YongPyong, the XCO and XCC at Les Gets and the XCO at Lake Placid, where she also secured the XCC overall title.
- In Val di Sole on 30 August, Frei won the **elite women's XCO world title**. RockShox's World Series coverage describes her bike as her "World Championship winning Epic 9".
- The Epic 8 ended its career on top: Christopher Blevins won the 2025 XCO and XCC World Cup overalls.

## What do the first reviews say?

- **Pinkbike** frames it as a pure race bike before anything else.
- **Bikerumor** calls it an excellent successor to the Epic 8 in almost every respect, with price as the only real drawback.
- **Singletracks** finds it more race-oriented than earlier short-travel Epics but slightly plusher than the Epic 8, and reaching its limits on steep descents.
- **BIKE** tested the Expert and rates it the sweet spot of the range.

## The verdict

The Epic 9 is a careful update. It brings a lighter frame, less friction and the same 120 mm platform. Its race record in 2026 makes the case better than any spec sheet. At $15,249.99, though, the S-Works is a bike for racers with a sponsor or a large budget. BIKE's verdict points to the Expert at $7,499.99, which uses the same frame shape.

*Prices and weights come from Specialized, Pinkbike, Bikerumor, Velomotion, Singletracks and BIKE. Race results come from the UCI, Brújula and RockShox.*

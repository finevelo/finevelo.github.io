Cervélo's Áspero-5 is the brand's gravel race bike, and its case rests on aerodynamics. The top build with SRAM Red AXS costs $12,650, complete bikes start at $8,850, and a frameset is $5,500. Cervélo does not publish a complete-bike or frame weight, so that figure is TBC. The company says only that the new Áspero-5 is a claimed 66 grams lighter than its predecessor.

## What is the Áspero-5 for?

Cervélo says the bike was "built for the front end of the race". It made its debut under Marianne Vos as UCI Gravel World Champion, and Cervélo says she won on it. The practical spec is race-oriented but not stripped back: clearance for 45mm tires, a threaded bottom bracket, downtube storage and a frame that is compatible with direct-mount top-tube bags.

## How much faster does Cervélo say it is?

Cervélo calls the Áspero-5 "the most aerodynamic gravel bike ever created". Its figures:

- a 37-watt saving over the outgoing Áspero
- a 34-watt advantage over the closest rival bike
- almost 6 watts credited to the aero handlebar and stem alone

The product page does not give test speed, yaw angles or tire setup. These are manufacturer claims that cannot be compared directly with other brands' numbers. Cervélo also says the new bike is 66 grams lighter than the old one, even though it has more surface area for aerodynamics.

## What does the mixed-tire geometry mean?

This is the most unusual idea on the bike. Cervélo says that with equal tires, for example 42mm front and rear, handling is stable and suited to rough courses. But the geometry has been optimized for a 4mm difference between front and rear, such as 40mm front and 44mm rear. Cervélo says this setup improves aerodynamics further and sharpens handling on less technical courses.

The downtube storage comes from the P-Series triathlon bike. Cervélo says it takes a tube, tools and a flat kit. The brand claims no other system on the market is lighter or more aerodynamic.

## How much does it cost?

All complete builds are 1x 12-speed with a power meter, a ceramic bottom bracket, carbon cockpit and seatpost, and Reserve 48/53GR wheels.

The frameset includes the carbon handlebar, stem and seatpost. That matters because the cockpit accounts for part of the claimed aero saving. Cervélo notes that prices and specs are subject to change.

## What does the geometry look like?

Six sizes are offered. The product page has "Balanced" and "Mixed" geometry views; the figures below are those Cervélo publishes.

| Size | Reach (mm) | Stack (mm) | Head angle | Seat angle | Wheelbase (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 48 | 369 | 500 | 70.6° | 74.1° | 993 |
| 51 | 377 | 525 | 71.1° | 73.6° | 1002 |
| 54 | 386 | 550 | 71.6° | 73.1° | 1012 |
| 56 | 395 | 575 | 71.6° | 72.6° | 1029 |
| 58 | 404 | 600 | 71.6° | 72.6° | 1047 |
| 61 | 413 | 625 | 71.6° | 72.6° | 1064 |

Chainstays are 422.5mm across all sizes. Bottom-bracket drop is 80mm on the four smaller sizes and 78mm on the 58 and 61. Fork offset is 57.5mm on the 48, 54.5mm on the 51 and 51.5mm from size 54 up.

## Who is it for?

On Cervélo's own terms, this is a bike for riders who race gravel and want to save watts. For them, a power meter, deep wheels and an aero cockpit are part of the brief. The 45mm limit and the split-tire geometry point to fast courses rather than loaded touring. The GRX Di2 build gives Shimano riders a route in at $8,850. The $5,500 frameset with cockpit suits anyone moving over an existing groupset and wheels.

We have not ridden the Áspero-5, so the aero and handling claims above are Cervélo's alone.

Zipp's 353 NSW Tubeless Disc-brake is the brand's lightest wheelset and, by Zipp's own account, its most advanced. It is also the first wheelset to connect to SRAM's AXS system. Zipp lists an MSRP of $1,950–$2,350 and a claimed weight of 1,310 g in the lightest configuration (12 mm thru-axles and XDR body, tape and valves included).

That price sits below where we normally start with wheelsets. The reason for a closer look is the sensor built into the rim: tyre-pressure monitoring that lives inside the wheel rather than on the valve stem.

## What does the AXS Wheel Sensor actually do?

Zipp says the sensor allows real-time pressure monitoring and post-ride analysis without an external device fitted to the valve. Before a ride, an LED gives a quick status check. During the ride, Zipp names Hammerhead Karoo as the head unit for live readings, so riders can fine-tune pressure and spot potential issues early.

| LED signal | Meaning (per Zipp) |
|---|---|
| Slow green flashing | Pressure within the normal/suggested range |
| Slow red flashing | Low pressure detected |
| Fast red flashing | High pressure detected |

Zipp's feature list also notes that pressure can be monitored when running a "specially designed tube", so the system is not limited to sealant setups.

## What changed in the rim and hub?

Zipp says a revised carbon layup raises rim impact strength by more than 16 percent. The rim keeps the undulating Sawtooth profile, varying between 35 and 40 mm deep, with Hyperfoil nodes and the HexFin ABLC dimple pattern, which Zipp credits with aero and crosswind-stability performance. Zipp also lists its TSE technology for reduced rolling resistance.

The ZR1 SL hubset uses hybrid ceramic bearings and 66 points of engagement. Zipp says it is 30 g lighter than the hub previously used in NSW wheels.

## How much does it cost?

Zipp lists the 353 NSW at $1,950–$2,350 MSRP. The product page does not break down which option sits at which end of that range. Riders on Campagnolo will need the separately sold driver body. A Center Lock lockring is included, and the wheels carry Zipp's lifetime warranty.

## Who is it for?

Zipp aims the 353 NSW at endurance riders and road racers, and describes it as a lightweight climber's wheel. Zipp pitches the 35/40 mm depth and 30 mm tyre optimisation at varied tarmac riding: valley roads, mountain climbs and descents.

The limits matter. Zipp states the wheel is not approved for gravel riding. As a hookless rim, it is capped at 73 psi and requires TSS-compatible tubeless tyres. Zipp points to the Goodyear Fitment Series, made exclusively for Zipp. The maximum system weight is 115 kg.

The sensor is the deciding factor. For riders who already track pressure closely, and especially those using a Hammerhead Karoo, integrated monitoring removes a valve-mounted gadget and adds data. For riders who set pressure once and forget it, the case rests on the low claimed weight and the revised rim and hub.

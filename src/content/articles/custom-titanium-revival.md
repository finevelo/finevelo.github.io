Handmade titanium has crept up to carbon-superbike prices. Moots' new **County Line** frameset costs **$5,499**, and complete bikes run from **$9,999 to $12,999**. A Mosaic AR-Zero frameset costs **$11,000**, Baum's DBM 32 frame kit starts at **AU$13,450**. That is superbike money, for a frame built in a small workshop, often to one rider's measurements.

Titanium framesets are rarely sold by weight. None of the builder pages we checked lists a frame weight for its custom models.

## What is new in 2026?

- **Moots County Line.** Moots launched an all-road bike in October. It is designed around 38 mm tyres, clears 42 mm and comes with a new carbon fork. Completes start at $9,999 with SRAM Force; a lighter RSL version with double-butted tubing follows in early 2027.
- **Baum DBM 32.** The Australian builder's custom gravel bike rolls on 32-inch wheels, a size usually seen on mountain bikes.
- **Eddy Merckx moved into titanium.** The Belgian brand added titanium versions of its Corsa Pévèle and Corsa Strasbourg, built in Italy from Columbus Hyperion tubing (framesets £4,499 / €4,999).
- **3D printing keeps spreading.** Since 2023, J.Laverack's AM64 frames, Sturdy's printed junctions and Firefly's printed fork crowns and dropouts have combined printed titanium parts with drawn tubes.

## Why is titanium not easy money?

Two events this year show how exposed small metal builders are:

- **Lynskey filed for Chapter 11 on 30 April 2026.** The Tennessee titanium maker's filing cited raw titanium costs up about 40% since 2021 (as SGB Media reported from the court documents; Bicycle Retailer and Escape Collective covered the filing).
- **Atherton Bikes' factory caught fire on 11 October 2026.** The Welsh brand builds mountain bikes, including its A-Range models with 3D-printed titanium lugs and carbon tubes. Nobody was hurt, but Atherton expects production and deliveries to be disrupted.

Custom builders have responded by adding stock or batch-built lines, which shortens lead times and keeps workshops busy. Examples are Mosaic's Batch Built programme and Sturdy's off-the-peg SC-G.

## Is custom titanium worth it?

Price is not where titanium wins. A custom frame fits one rider, and bare, brushed titanium needs no paint to protect it. The trade-off is weight and stiffness at a given price, where the best carbon frames remain ahead. If you want a bike you can keep for decades, titanium is a rational choice. If you want to race, it is a romantic one.

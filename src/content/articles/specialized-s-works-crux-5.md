The fifth-generation Crux was launched on 28 May 2026. The **S-Works Crux 5 costs $14,000 / €13,999 / £11,999**, which is $2,000 more than the S-Works Crux 4 at its 2021 launch. Specialized claims about **7.1 kg** for a complete bike in size 56 and **789 g** for the painted S-Works frame in size 56.

That frame is 64 g heavier than its predecessor, a notable step for a bike that won its reputation on weight. In return, Specialized claims the new Crux is **15.2 W faster at 45 km/h** and clears **55 mm tyres**.

The S-Works build runs SRAM Red XPLR AXS as a 1x drivetrain only, with chainrings up to 52T. It also gets a Roval Terra Aero integrated cockpit and 45 mm Specialized Pathfinder tyres.

The new Roval Terra Aero CLX wheels are 50 mm deep with a 27 mm internal width and carbon spokes. In the US and Australia only, a FACT 10r frameset is also sold for $2,500.

## How much does it weigh?

Claimed complete weights vary slightly between sources:

- **Claimed by Specialized, size 56:** 7.1 kg (Gran Fondo quotes the same figure for a size 54 with 45 mm tyres). BikeRadar says stock builds start at 7.2 kg.
- **Measured by Cyclist:** 7.6 kg for a size 58 S-Works, with pedals, bottle cages and a computer mount fitted.
- **Lightest launch build:** BikeRadar saw about 6.64 kg, with narrower tyres and lighter Terra CLX III wheels.

## What's new vs. the previous generation?

The Crux 4 was a climbing bike that happened to fit gravel tyres. The Crux 5 borrows its tube shapes from the Tarmac SL7 and SL8 road bikes. Specialized splits the 15.2 W aero claim as follows:

- about half from the frame, fork and seatpost
- 30% from the wheels
- 20% from the cockpit

The claim comes from wind-tunnel testing with a mannequin. Specialized also calculated that Sofía Gómez Villafañe would have finished Unbound 2025 almost ten minutes sooner on the new bike. That is a simulation, not a result.

Other changes:

- **Geometry:** the bottom bracket is 6 mm lower and the head angle 0.5° slacker (71.5° on a 56). The seat angle is 0.5° steeper. Reach grows by 3 to 10 mm on sizes 56 to 61, and chainstays stay at 425 mm.
- **Cable routing** is fully internal through the headset on the S-Works and S-Level builds. The seatpost is the D-shaped post from the Tarmac SL8.
- **There is still no internal frame storage.** Instead there are new mounts on the top tube and fork legs.

## Has it won anything?

The new bike's first major race went its way. At Unbound Gravel 200 on 30 May 2026, both winners, Mads Würtz Schmidt and Sofía Gómez Villafañe, rode the Crux 5. According to Velo, so did both runners-up. Velo also notes the obvious caveat: Specialized sponsors many of the fastest riders.

The previous generation ended its career with a world title. Lorena Wiebes won the 2025 UCI Gravel World Championships on an S-Works Crux.

The Crux is the most expensive bike in this group and also the lightest. The most direct challenge so far came on 7 October: Colnago's new G5-X race gravel bike has a claimed 950 g frame, clears 53 mm tyres and costs around €9,200 with SRAM Red XPLR. In the US it lists at $10,500, according to Velo and Road Bike Action.

## What the first reviews say

- **Cyclist** finds it more capable and faster than before and almost as light, with better gravel geometry. It is still stiff, especially on the deep Terra Aero wheels, and Cyclist calls the price hard to justify.
- **BikeRadar** rode the S-Level build and called it a hugely impressive race bike. It noted a firm front end and suggested the cheaper builds or the frameset make more sense for most buyers.
- **Gran Fondo** praises its acceleration and composure at speed. It criticises the stock 40T chainring as too small for racing and regrets the lack of internal storage.

## The verdict

The Crux 5 trades a little of what made its predecessor special (the lowest frame weight in the class) for speed on the flat and room for 55 mm tyres. For racing, that is the right trade: at gravel race speeds, drag and tyre volume matter more than a few dozen grams of frame weight.

At $14,000, though, the S-Works is hard to recommend over the **S-Level at $10,500**. The S-Level uses the same frame shape, made in FACT 10r carbon, and the same Red XPLR groupset. It weighs only about 0.6 kg more (7.7 kg claimed).

Cervélo's R5 is the brand's climbing bike, and its headline figure is a claimed 5.97kg for a size 56 in SRAM Red and Shimano Dura-Ace builds. Complete bikes run from $10,100 to $14,400, and the frameset costs $6,500. Launch date: TBC.

That is 900g under the UCI's 6.8kg minimum, so pros cannot race it at that weight. Cervélo's point is that riders outside the pro peloton can benefit from what it calls "the lightest road bike on the market". Treat that as the manufacturer's claim, not a measured ranking.

## What is the R5 for?

Cervélo positions the R5 for high mountains and steep grades, naming the Tour de France and Vuelta a España as its natural terrain. Cervélo says Team Visma | Lease a Bike switches between the R5 and the aero S5. It also says Pauline Ferrand-Prévot rode the R5 to victory at "this summer's" Tour de France Femmes.

The brief, as the brand frames it, is a light frame that is still stiff enough for hard accelerations and that descends predictably.

## Where does the weight saving come from?

Cervélo gives a claimed 651g frame and 298g fork in size 56. It says the frame and fork together are 100g lighter than before. Across all Cervélo-made parts, it claims a total reduction of 326g.

The largest single contributor is the HB18 one-piece bar and stem. Cervélo says it saves nearly 150g and makes the bike two watts more aerodynamic than the previous R5. Purpose-designed Reserve 34|37 SL wheels account for another claimed 60g. Cervélo says a refined layup saves that weight while keeping the strength and stiffness of the previous wheels. The build listings name the wheels as Reserve 34|37TA, so it is worth confirming the exact wheel with a dealer.

The three top builds have ceramic bottom brackets, and every complete bike comes with a power meter. The frameset weight includes the cockpit and carbon seatpost. The listed weights do not line up exactly with the 5.97kg headline. Cervélo notes that weights vary by size, spec and paint, and that prices and specs may change.

The $250 gap between Red and Dura-Ace is small. The step down to Force or Ultegra saves about $4,000. Those builds use DT Swiss 240 hubs instead of 180s and have no ceramic bottom bracket.

## What changed in the geometry?

Cervélo says handling geometry is almost unchanged. The exception is a small bottom bracket drop adjustment to suit 29mm tyres. Fit geometry now matches the S5, which matters to riders who switch between the two. Size 56 figures:

Six sizes run from 48 to 61.

## Who should consider it?

The R5 suits riders who do most of their riding on long climbs and want a racing position shared with Cervélo's aero bike. The one-piece cockpit is the main practical consideration. Cervélo offers 15 size combinations and says its authorised retailers will help select the right one at no extra cost. That service is worth using, because bar width and stem length cannot be changed separately on a one-piece cockpit.

We have not ridden the R5. The figures above are Cervélo's.

Merida's Reacto Team sits in an unusual spot within an aero range: it is the model built around weight. Merida claims 7.1 kg for the complete bike and approximately 950 g for the CF5 frame in size M. It quotes 203 watts in Tour Magazine's wind-tunnel test. The global product page gives no price, so that figure is TBC for now.

## What is the Reacto Team?

The Team belongs to the fifth generation of the Reacto, which Merida calls Generation V. Merida describes it as "the lightest model" in that line-up. It is aimed at riders who prioritise weight but do not want to give up aerodynamic performance.

The name has history. Merida says a Team version has been in the range since the Reacto first appeared in 2011.

## How does it fit into the Reacto range?

Alongside the Team, Merida's Reacto collection lists the 10K, ONE and PRO, plus the 9000 to 4000 models. Merida positions the latest bikes by focus:

- **Reacto ONE:** the low-drag benchmark. Merida says it "substantially undercuts" 200 watts on Tour's test standard.
- **Reacto Team:** the low-weight option, at a quoted 203 watts.
- **Other models:** described by Merida as a balance of aerodynamics, comfort and control.

By Merida's own description, the Team gives up some drag against the ONE in exchange for the lowest weight in the family. Merida does not publish a watt figure for the ONE, so the exact gap is TBC.

## What's new in this generation?

According to Merida, Generation V builds on the previous Reacto with three changes:

- improved aerodynamics
- reduced weight
- tyre clearance of up to 32 mm

Merida says the comfort and control of earlier Reactos have been kept.

Two named technologies feature on the frame:

- **S-Flex seatpost:** a carbon aero post with a flex section. Spare-parts lists show versions with and without the flex part.
- **Disc Cooler:** Merida lists Disc Cooler parts for both the front and rear brake mounts.

## What is it built with?

The build centres on Shimano Dura-Ace and Vision components:

- **Drivetrain:** Dura-Ace Di2 12-speed, a dual-sided power meter on the 52-36 crankset, and an 11-30 cassette.
- **Wheels:** Vision Metron 60 RS, 60 mm deep with a 23 mm inner width. They are tubeless ready, but tape and valves are not included.
- **Cockpit:** a one-piece Vision Metron 5D ACR EVO bar and stem.

## What does the geometry look like?

The Team comes in seven sizes. Every size shares 410 mm chainstays and a 45 mm fork offset. Stack-to-reach ratios run from 1.37 to 1.45, which puts the bike on the race-oriented side.

| Size | Stack (mm) | Reach (mm) | Head angle | Seat angle | Wheelbase (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| XS | 529 | 384 | 72° | 74° | 984 |
| M | 557 | 395 | 73.5° | 73.5° | 992 |
| XL | 593 | 409 | 74° | 73° | 1011 |

Cockpit sizing scales with the frame. Stems run from 90 mm on the three smallest sizes to 120 mm on the XL.

## Who is it for?

Merida pitches the Team at riders whose focus is weight but who still want aerodynamic performance. For the lowest drag in the range, Merida points to the Reacto ONE instead.

We have not ridden the bike, and Merida has not published pricing on its global page. Availability and specification can also vary by country, as Merida notes that its distributors tailor the range to each market.

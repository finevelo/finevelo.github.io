Baum Cycles' DBM is a titanium drop-bar bike designed around mountain-bike tyres up to 29 x 2.40in. Baum lists the frame package from AUD 13,450 and a complete bike from AUD 16,192. It does not publish a weight, so that figure is TBC. Baum also offers a 32-inch wheel configuration as an option through its Tailored Package.

## What exactly is the DBM?

Baum calls it an adventure gravel bike and explains the concept plainly: the front end of a gravel bike joined to the rear end of an XC bike. The aim is to make room for tyres that would normally belong on a cross-country mountain bike.

The company positions it as a rougher-terrain sibling of its XTM, and is explicit that it is not a "drop-bar conversion". Baum says fit and handling sit in line with its other gravel bikes, while ride quality and refinement are carried over from the Orbis.

## What makes the frame different?

Most of the engineering is at the back. Baum says a 3D-printed yoke works with an 89.5 mm T47 bottom bracket shell to clear XC tyres. That shell takes MTB cranks only.

The rear end uses Baum's proprietary 6/4 titanium thru-axle dropouts, revised to accept SRAM's Universal Derailleur Hanger and its T-Type/Full Mount derailleurs. Axle spacing is 148 x 12 mm Boost.

## Where do the 32-inch wheels come in?

The 32-inch option sits in the Tailored Package. That package also covers custom fitting, personalised steering and handling, bespoke tube selection and butting, a suspension fork, rack eyelets, a lunch box mount, a name on the top tube or chainstay, and non-standard colours. Baum does not publish clearance or geometry figures specific to 32-inch wheels.

Integration upgrades are separate: Baum's Orbis Integration hides the brake hoses using a Chris King Aeroset headset and ENVE IN-Route steering components.

## How is the geometry set up?

Baum says the DBM avoids the slack head angle, steep seat angle and high bottom bracket of an XC bike. It describes the geometry as the Orbis X adapted for bigger tyres. Figures below are Baum's, in mm and degrees.

Frames come with short and tall headset caps and an offset seatpost. Baum will substitute a straight post, or stems 10 mm longer or shorter, on request.

## What does the money buy?

The AUD 13,450 base package includes the titanium frame, fork, headset, ENVE stem and seatpost, and all paint schemes. The complete bike starts at AUD 16,192, with the build specified by the customer. Baum says "the rest of the specs are up to you", and handles build questions through a booked consultation.

## Who is it for?

On Baum's description, it suits riders who want drop bars and gravel-bike fit but whose routes run to rocky, steep climbs rather than smooth gravel. The MTB-only crank requirement and 1x limit point it towards off-road use. The tailored options make it a candidate for long, self-supported trips. We have not ridden it.

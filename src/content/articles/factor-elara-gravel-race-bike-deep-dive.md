The Factor Elara is the speed-focused model in Factor's gravel range, which the brand splits into ALUTO (race balance), Sarana (race endurance) and Elara (race speed). Prices start at $6,599 for the Premium Package and reach $11,999 for the SRAM Red XPLR build. Factor claims the painted size M frame weighs under 1,100 g.

## What is the Elara for?

Factor describes it as a "high-speed gravel race system" aimed at the UCI Gravel World Series, the Gravel Earth Series and the World Championships. The stated goal is road-level race speed on rough ground. This is not a bikepacking machine with extra mounts; it is a race bike that happens to clear very large tyres.

## Why 57 mm tyres on a race bike?

According to Factor, the project began as an OSTRO Gravel update. Input from Magnus Bak Klaris, the 2025 Gravel Earth Series winner, reset the brief: the bike had to take 57 mm tyres front and rear. Factor says its engineers first considered that excessive. They then concluded that at race speeds larger tyres reduce rolling resistance on broken surfaces and lower puncture risk. A prototype was ready for race testing within four months, and Factor reports that Bak Klaris and Romain Bardet found it noticeably faster.

The full 57 mm clearance applies to 1× drivetrains, and Factor says the chainstays also clear a 52T 1× chainring. With a front derailleur, Factor quotes 47 mm.

## What's different from the OSTRO Gravel?

Factor claims a 5–8% lower drag coefficient than the OSTRO Gravel. It adds that CFD work confirms measurable drag reductions with 45–57 mm race tyres. The front end borrows from the ONE and OSTRO VAM programmes: a narrow hourglass head tube, planar fork integration and reworked transitions around the down tube and bottle zones. The fork has a deeper profile inspired by the ONE fork.

The frame is a true monocoque rather than a front triangle bonded to the stays. Factor says this allows chainstays thin enough for the tyre clearance and uses less material than a bonded construction. According to Factor, the new frame sheds weight compared with the outgoing one, and its own measurements show added stiffness around both the head tube and the bottom bracket.

Compared with its predecessor, the geometry has longer reach, reduced trail and a lower bottom bracket. Factor also cites a steeper seat tube angle that puts the rider in a more forward position.

Factor's page does not itemise the Premium Package, so check with a retailer what it includes. Factor says the bike comes in several colours; the product page shows Virescent Blue and Matte Carbon.

Factor presents the bike alongside Black Inc parts. The AB07 Aero Gravel Bar is a one-piece cockpit with slight flare, an integrated computer mount and internal hose routing. The SIXTY FOUR Gravel wheelset requires a minimum 47 mm tyre.

## Who is it for?

It suits riders who race gravel at the front and come from road racing. Factor says the position should feel familiar to a road racer, with 420–425 mm chainstays keeping the handling quick. Riders who want a less race-focused gravel bike will find that Factor positions the Sarana as its race-endurance option.

We have not ridden the Elara, so we cannot say whether the aero and tyre-size claims translate into speed on course. On paper, it shows where top-level gravel racing is heading: road geometry, aero tubes and much wider rubber.

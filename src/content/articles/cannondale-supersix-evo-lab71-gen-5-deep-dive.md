Cannondale's fifth-generation SuperSix Evo is offered in two LAB71 builds built around its Series 0 carbon frame. The headline figure is a claimed 755 g for a fully painted 56cm frame, paired with a claimed 378 g fork. Cannondale's US product pages do not list prices or complete-bike weights for either LAB71 build, so both remain TBC.

## What is the LAB71 SuperSix Evo?

Both LAB71 builds use what the brand calls Series 0 carbon, which it describes as its lightest and most advanced construction to date, combining carbon fibre with a "nano-resin" layup. Cannondale says each frame size gets its own layup and geometry so that stiffness and handling stay consistent from small to large.

The brand positions the bike as a lightweight race machine that also competes on drag. It claims the bike beats many dedicated aero bikes while weighing "up to a kilo less". Cannondale presents the standard LAB71 build as equipment fit for EF Pro Cycling.

## What changed from Gen 4?

According to Cannondale:

- Drag is reduced across the yaw range compared with Gen 4, developed through CFD and wind-tunnel work. No figures are given.
- Stack is 10mm lower in every size.
- The old size 51 is replaced by a 50 and a 52, giving smaller steps at the small end.

## What else does the frame offer?

The frame uses a BSA 68mm threaded bottom bracket, 12x142mm rear axle, flat-mount discs, an integrated seat binder and a UDH derailleur hanger. Fork offset is 55mm on 44-54cm frames and 45mm on 56-61cm. On tyres, Cannondale says the biggest it has measured in the frame is 32mm, and that this still leaves no less than 4mm of space either side.

Cannondale's Delta steerer (1-1/8" to 1-1/4") routes cables internally. Seatpost hardware is titanium. Cannondale says the included aero bottles and cages were designed with the frame to reduce drag while still accepting round bottles.

## How do the two LAB71 builds differ?

The frameset is shared. The SL wears a minimalist raw finish that Cannondale says saves further weight, though it gives no number.

## How much does it cost?

Cannondale's product pages, as provided, do not state a price for either build, so we list both as TBC. Both specs include flagship groupsets, ceramic-bearing hubs and power meters as standard. That places them at the top of the market, but we will not guess at a figure.

## Who is each build for?

Cannondale is explicit. The SL is for "the truly weight-obsessed", with steep climbs and summit finishes in mind. It gets shallow 38mm wheels with carbon spokes, which Cannondale calls DT Swiss's lightest aero wheelset, and an ultralight, wider SystemBar Road SL cockpit.

The standard LAB71 is pitched at pure race speed. Cannondale lists it for breakaways, KOMs and podiums. Its deeper Reserve 57/64 wheels, which Cannondale bills for breakaways and sprints, sit alongside the aero-shaped SystemBar Road with 20mm of flare per side and bigger 52/36 gearing.

Because the frame is shared, the choice between them comes down to wheels, groupset preference and riding priorities. We have not ridden either, and will reserve judgement until Cannondale confirms prices and complete weights.
